Please also explain why a resell market naturally emerges when the original box office prices are set too low, ensuring the entire explanation remains simple and free of mathematical formulas or complex economic jargon.

Imagine your favorite band is coming to town, and they are playing a concert in a huge stadium. Everyone wants to go see them live! This situation helps us understand how prices work using two big ideas: Supply and Demand.

The Limit on Seats (Scarcity)

First, let’s talk about the size of the stadium. Even though it is very big, it only has a certain number of seats. Maybe there are 50,000 chairs in total. No matter how many people want to buy tickets, there will never be more than 50,000 available.

In economics, this reason for having fewer things than people want is called Scarcity. Because the concert hall has a limit on seats, the supply of tickets cannot grow once the event starts. There simply are not enough chairs to go around for every single fan who wants one.

Everyone Wants Tickets (Demand)

Now think about how many fans are willing to buy a ticket. If 100,000 people want to go, but there are only 50,000 tickets, that is called high Demand. Demand is just the number of people who really want to buy something at a certain price.

The Sweet Spot Price (Equilibrium)

So, how much should the ticket cost? This brings us to Equilibrium. You can think of equilibrium as the “sweet spot” price. It is the perfect middle ground where everything balances out.

  • If tickets are too expensive, like $1,000 each, most people will say, “That costs too much!” and they won’t buy them. The seats might sit empty.
  • If tickets are too cheap, like $5 each, everyone will rush to the store at the same time. Since there is high demand but limited supply (Scarcity), all 50,000 tickets would sell out in seconds.

The Equilibrium price is just right. It is a price where enough people are willing to pay so that every seat gets filled, but not so many people show up that they can’t get in. At this sweet spot, the number of buyers matches the number of available seats perfectly.

Why a Resell Market Appears

Sometimes, the organizers make a mistake and set the box office price too low. Let’s say tickets cost $20 each at the official store, but everyone thinks they are worth way more because the band is so popular.

Because the price is so cheap, thousands of people try to buy them all at once. The 50,000 tickets sell out in minutes. However, there are still thousands of fans who didn’t get a ticket and really want one. They will pay extra money just to see their favorite band perform.

This creates a natural Resell Market. People who managed to buy tickets for $20 realize they could make more profit by selling them online for $100 or $200. Since the original price was too low, these fans and sellers start trading tickets privately at higher prices until the market finds its own “sweet spot” again. This happens naturally because people want to trade what is valuable (the ticket) for money they can use elsewhere.
